Keyfari

Click anything on your Mac from the keyboard

Press a shortcut and every button, link and menu item gets a short label. Type the label to click it. It works in Mission Control too. Free and open source.

Download for macOS View the source

Requires macOS 14 or later. Apple silicon and Intel.

It also reads what apps do not expose

Some windows tell the system nothing about their contents: canvas-drawn interfaces, games, remote desktops. Keyfari reads the pixels on your Mac and labels the text it finds. Here the window is a picture of a made-up app.

Three modes, three shortcuts

All of them can be changed in the settings.

⌘⇧Space

Hints

Labels on everything clickable. Hold Shift on the last letter for a right click, Command for a double click.

⌘⇧/

Search

Type part of an element's text, then Return. Chinese text matches its pinyin, in full or as initials.

⌘⇧J

Scroll

H J K L to scroll, D and U for half a page, G G for the top. Tab switches between scroll areas.

Made to stay out of the way

Keyfari lives in the menu bar and does nothing until you call it.

  • Works with any input method. Keys are read by position, so a Chinese or Japanese input method does not get in the way.
  • Does not steal focus. Open menus and popovers stay open and get labels too.
  • Private. No telemetry. The only network requests are for updates, which can be switched off. Text detection runs on the device.
  • Stable labels. An element keeps the label you last clicked it with, or one you choose yourself.
  • Mission Control. Its windows and desktops get labels too, on every display. Type one to go there.
  • Desktops. Optional shortcuts take you one desktop to the left or right.
  • See what it saved you. Clicks, streaks, most used apps and the time saved, counted on your Mac only.
  • Yours to shape. Shortcuts, label letters, themes, scroll speed, ignored apps.
  • Three languages. English, Chinese and Japanese.

A settings window worth opening

Change a theme or a size and the preview shows the result at once.

Install

Keyfari is not signed with an Apple Developer ID, so macOS blocks it the first time you open it.

Download

  1. Get the latest disk image and drag Keyfari to Applications.
  2. Open it once. macOS will refuse.
  3. Go to System Settings > Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway.
  4. Grant the Accessibility permission when asked.

Homebrew

brew install --cask hodgekou/keyfari/keyfari

The same first-launch step applies. macOS asks for the permissions again after each update.
